## **Introduction (1500 words)**

At its core, Christianity is interpersonal. The Christian life is experienced in community with others. While we enjoy a personal relationship with God, the life of faith is not an individual endeavor. When God calls us to himself, he also invites us to forge deep relationships with other believers. The shared life and common bond of this Christian fellowship is one of the great blessings of the Christian life. In addition to fellowship with other believers, God has placed each of us in various spheres of life in which we have encounters and interactions with other humans. In an effort to mature us in our faith, God wants to shape the way we live out our faith in each of these interpersonal spheres. An introduction to Christian relationships, The Bond Between Us lays out a pathway that helps Christians order their various relationships after God’s design.

Exploring the essentials of a Christian approach to relationships is important for many reasons. For one, new believers often bring the baggage of past relationships into their newly found Christian lives. While we pledge our faith to Christ and seek to reform our conduct, we also must allow our relationships with others to undergo a transformation. God wants to reorder our interactions with others after his design. Additionally, these reordered relationships offer a compelling witness to the surrounding world. It is often through interpersonal relationships that the gospel is shared and people come to faith in Christ. As unbelievers observe the transformation in the life of a disciple—in addition to the transformation that occurs in a community of disciples—this becomes a powerful testimony to the Christian faith.

Christian community is one of God’s great aims in his global mission. To become mature in our faith, we need to establish foundations of correct belief, godly living, and formative habits. No Christian can be mature without deep moorings sunk into these areas of the Christian faith. These pillars of the faith have been explored in previous books of the Narrative Journey series. As these foundations are laid, we must also learn how to interact with others in a godly manner. Mature Christian faith orders, calibrates, and defines its relationships after God’s design. Not only are the essentials of the faith meant to be embraced individually as a disciple, but these essentials are also meant to be embodied as a community. God’s mission is to establish mature communities of faith throughout the world.

Further, foundations of Christian community also provide a context where Christian formation can occur. Cultivating healthy relationships in various spheres of life allows for fertile surroundings in which to grow our faith. Interacting with family members challenges our Christian character. Ordering our parenting and reforming our marriages after God’s design is crucial to embodying the faith as a mature disciple. Participating in a loving Christian community provides support for our faith journey. Meaningfully and redemptively interacting with those outside of the Christian community will galvanize our own faith as Christians. Redefining how we approach work and personal resources puts our faith to the test on a daily basis. Even reconsidering how Christians should interact with government and public life proves to be helpful for our formation as Christians. In short, establishing foundations of Christian relationships equips believers to put their faith into practice in concrete and practical ways.

In this study, we offer twelve biblical narratives that deal with various aspects of Christian relationships. The stories are divided into two main groups. The first set of six stories will address relationships connected with the Christian family and surrounding society. The second set of six stories addresses spheres of relationship involving the church community. Broadly speaking, these two sets of stories relate to each other in dynamic ways. While the family is a basic unit of relationships for individual development and society, the church is God’s redeemed people operating as a family of faith.

Connected together, the biblical passages form a pathway by which we can mature in our approach to Christian relationships. Aiming to establish godly families, we explore six passages that orient believers to foundations of community life in a Christian household. The journey taken through these six stories teaches believers how to live in the context of family (Genesis 15:1–19:38), marriage (Genesis 24:1–67), parenting (1 Samuel 1:1–3:21), friendship (1 Samuel 18:1–20:42), government (Daniel 1:1–2:49; 4:1–6:28), and work (Nehemiah 1:1–7:4). Building off of the foundations of godly family life, we explore six more stories related to Christian community, now considering the context of the church. Through these six stories, Christians learn to relate to Jesus as a family of faith (Mark 3:7–35), care for one another with community virtues (Matthew 17:22–18:35), practice a common life by caring for the needy of the community (Acts 4:32–6:7), understand relationship with leaders and other churches (Acts 14:19–16:5), interact with domains of the surrounding world (Acts 16:11–18:17), and practice hospitality to outsiders (Acts 27:1–28:31).

God has placed each of us in various spheres of influence. These relationships are testing grounds where our faith is put into practice. The bond we enjoy with other humans is one of the great mysteries and joys of God’s created world. In the Fall, these relationships became places of pain, hardship, brokenness, and abuse. In Christ, God powerfully redeems and transforms the way we approach our relationships with one another. In the very call of discipleship, Jesus wants us to consider how we can order each domain and relationship after God’s design. Our maturity of faith is reflected by our willingness to do just this.

Discussing various domains of human relationships is not easy. Humans disappoint one another. People fail to live up to expectations. Loved ones can place inappropriate and unhealthy expectations on others. Even with our best intentions, we can hurt, disappoint, or frustrate. Needless to say, we venture into a conversation about a Christian view of relationships not because it is easy, but because it is close to the heart of the faith. Christianity is an interpersonal faith in which we enjoy our relationship with God in the context of relationship with others. As we consider what it means to live rightly within the spheres of family, society, work, church, and the unbelieving world, we can once again experience God’s powerful and gracious work to redeem what otherwise would be impossible to redeem. In the study that follows, we hope that God’s vision of mature relationships—“the bond between us” in various spheres of life—will come to reality as we journey toward Christian community.

### **Core Content**

An outline of Christian teaching is like a map for the journey of faith. Survey the twelve scriptural passages and associated themes that will set the itinerary for your upcoming study.

Session 1: The Household (Genesis 15:1–19:38) Abraham and Sarah show the struggles and importance of family life.

Session 2: The Union (Genesis 24:1–67) Isaac and Rebekah lay solid foundations for a God-honoring marriage.

Session 3: The Offspring (1 Samuel 1:1–3:21) Hannah and Samuel exemplify godly habits in the parent-child relationship.

Session 4: The Bond (1 Samuel 18:1–20:42) Jonathan and David exhibit the importance of deep friendship as they love each other like brothers.

Session 5: The Public (Daniel 1:1–2:49; 4:1–6:28) Daniel navigates the political arena while maintaining his faith convictions.

Session 6: The Work (Nehemiah 1:1–7:4) Nehemiah lives out his vocation in a way that serves God’s purposes in the world.

Session 7: The Church (Mark 3:7–35 )Jesus forms the church as a faith family out of a diverse group of Christ followers.

Session 8: The Community (Matthew 17:22–18:35) Jesus grounds his people in the core virtues of community life.

Session 9: The Commons (Acts 4:32–6:7) The early church practices radical generosity in meeting the needs of its members.

Session 10: The Network (Acts 14:19–16:5) The early church establishes core leaders and connects itself as a network of churches as a means to participate in God’s global work.

Session 11: The Worlds (Acts 16:11–18:17) Paul exemplifies how to winsomely engage the world in its spiritual, economic, legal, civic (social and political), intellectual, cultural, and religious domains.

Session 12: The Other (Acts 27:1–28:31) Paul experiences and extends hospitality to show how Christians engage with those who are outsiders.

### **Project Preview**

A firm conviction of Christian truth is foundational to a mature faith. Therefore, at the end of this book, you will be asked to write a **creedal statement**. This creed is a condensed and memorable summary of the core biblical teachings on Christian belief. It is also a capstone project for this study. You can write this individually or as a community. The aim of this project is to help you synthesize all that you will learn over the course of this study as it relates to *the essential teachings of the Christian faith*. As we trek through the next twelve sessions, it will be important to keep this project in your mind.

The following criteria will help guide your thinking about how you (and your church community) might draft such a statement. The creedal statement should be

* **short**: summarizes the key biblical teachings in a condensed and accessible format

* **memorable**: articulates biblical principles in a way that is easily memorizable

* **usable**: expresses the creed as a statement that can be affirmed and confessed together as a church community

* **accurate**: makes correct, biblical affirmations of Christian belief

* **comprehensive**: addresses all major areas of Christian teaching
