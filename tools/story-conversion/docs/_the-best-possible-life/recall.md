## **Introduction**

The Christian life is a call to obedience. Emphasized throughout this study, genuine obedience is an outgrowth of authentic faith in Christ. As believers in Christ, we inherit eternal salvation through Jesus’ redemptive work on the cross. As followers of Christ, we live out our newfound freedom in submission to God’s will. Christ is our gracious Savior and sovereign Lord. His ways are our marching orders. His will governs our life purpose. His commands call us to virtuous character. His life on earth is our ultimate example and pattern for living. His word becomes our most important standard for faith and practice. A life of full and complete submission to Christ is nothing less than full fledged freedom. Although sinners can never attain salvation through their conduct, believers can enjoy God’s blessings through their obedient life. The freedom achieved through the gospel is experienced as we conform our lifestyle to God’s ways. Established and mature in the faith, Christian disciples ground their beliefs and shape their conduct after the truth of God’s revealed word.

The selected biblical passages in this study offer a brief outline of godly Christian living. In Ruth 1:1-4:22, we gain a broad picture of faithful living in the midst of hardship and suffering. Basic to the Christian life, we learn from Daniel 3:1-30 what it means to put God first in all of life through the courageous devotion of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. The catastrophic blunder in Exodus 32:1-35 cautions us about the dangers of coming to God on our own terms. It highlights the importance of pure worship and genuine devotion to God, unobstructed by idols and images. In Leviticus 8:1-10:20, the fateful defiance of Nadab and Abihu is contrasted with the godly reverence of Aaron and the priests, urging us to not treat God in common and profane ways. The record of Israel’s entrance into the promised land is recorded in Joshua 3:1-6:27. In this narrative, we learn about a life of rest, work, and community worship. We are called to honor God-appointed authorities through the biblical account of David, Saul, Abigail, and Nabal given in 1 Samuel 24:1-26:25. 

Believers understand the value of human life and learn our responsibility to our fellow humans through the story of Cain and Abel in Genesis 4:1-26. In Judges 13:1-16:31, we study about sexual purity through the repeated failures of Israel’s judge, Samson. The narrative of Naboth’s vineyard in 1 Kings 21:1-29 is a helpful story to instruct us in the dangers of theft. It also calls us to generosity, respect, and charity to one another. Nehemiah’s faithfulness and courage in Nehemiah 6:1-7:4 helps us understand the power of our words and calls us to godly speech. Israel’s unfaithfulness in Numbers 11:1-35 unveils the power of misplaced desires such as jealousy, covetousness, and greed. It calls us to a life of contentment and gratitude. Finally, the record of Queen Esther in Esther 1:1-10:3 helps us think about spiritual growth as a developmental journey.

God’s word is not only a record of his redemptive acts throughout history, it is also a guide for godly living. During the life of this Christian pilgrimage, we are called to reform our conduct after God’s ways. As we grow in godliness, we become reflections of God’s character to those around us. Such a transformation is not only evidence of a genuine faith; it is also a powerful witness to the watching world. Amazingly, our joy in God is connected to our faith, conduct, and witness. As genuine faith inevitably yields the fruit of godly living, so godly conduct exhibits God’s glory to others. Our joy—our experience of the best possible life—both in this life and in the life to come, is rooted a faithful, godly, and professing devotion to Christ.
